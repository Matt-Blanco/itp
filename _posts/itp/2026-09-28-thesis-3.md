### Connecting the Apple II

After two months of worth I finally achieved my goal of connecting a 40 year old computer to the internet. Over the week I continued iterating on my test network link driver. The first win was having the driver appear in a list of network connection options. (Except after selecting the option the operating system and computer would freeze). Fixing the errors involved finding source code for the networking software and comparing existing network connect in order to identify why my assembly code was causing the computer to crash.

The BlueSCSI device (the modern custom PCB allowing SD card storage and networking) was also having trouble connecting to the `sandbox370` WiFi so I used network sharing from my Mac in addition to testing on my phone's hotspot. Eventually, I had a version that was not crashing the computer, and supposedly was connected to WiFi though there was no way to know for sure without a test application.

So I wrote a test application. The program would ping my laptop and my laptop would ping back to test the data packet send and receive rates. The first several iterations both worked and didn't. The Apple II was not able to communicate with my Mac, but my Mac was able to send data to the Apple II with an 80% loss rate. After several iterations I realized the problem was due to my WiFi network. After connecting the BlueSCSI to my phone's hotspot the data loss rate dropped to 0-10% which is in the margin of error even for modern devices. The vintage computer can now be actually connected to a modern WiFi network and given an IP address to be readable by modern computers.

### Reading Notes

Over the past week I've continued reading as much as I can:
- [Medium is the Massage](https://archive.org/details/pdfy-vNiFct6b-L5ucJEa) by Marshall McLuhan
- [From Cybernation to Interaction: A Contribution to an Archeology of Interactivity](https://gebseng.com/media_archeology/reading_materials/Erkki_Huhtamo-Cybernation_to_Interaction.pdf) by Erkki Huhtamo
- [There is No Software](https://monoskop.org/images/f/f9/Kittler_Friedrich_1992_1997_There_Is_No_Software.pdf) by Friedrich Kittler

Each in there own way explore the methods that make code/software it's own type of media. McLuhan's tongue-in-cheeck version of his "Media is the message" was a beautiful book in it's writing and communication design that was also ahead of it's time. Even though the book was written around the 1970s, it's message felt fitting for today as well. With the introduction of electronic circuits and electronic media there was a large shift in how information was understood. Media, and especially electronic media, creates a new environment that we exist in representing a fundamental shift in knowledge transfer and communication among people.

Much of what I'm reading involved a new understanding of technology during times of change. New concepts and methodologies seem to stem from moments when culture and technology converge at the right moments. From McLuhan's time, the introduction of Wiring / Arduino, to AIs & LLMs bring an ontological shift in labor, technology, and creativity. What I'm thinking of now is what is the new ontology in a time when so much is shifting due to generative models.

My new belief is that LLMs are ushering in a new representation of generative media. We, as a technological society, have been practicing storing increasing amounts of data about our world. Now we've entered into a new space where alongside storing data about practically everything, we are generating data to simulate new environments alongside generative images, videos, text, etc. etc.
