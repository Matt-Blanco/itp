#### Research as Making 02 - A Stronger Theory?

At the beginning of the last class I thought of the term _Programmed Environments_, and immediately felt like it had weight to it. We exist in a series of environments: natural, urban, and rural. However, _Programmed Environments_ exist as a "meta-environment". Specifically in the ways code, as a mode of progrmamability, allow space to be abstracted. Over the past two weeks I spoke with Sarah and Nasif to get their thoughts, and if the theory makes sense.

Sarah summed the theory and my explorations on code as material in the following equation:

```bash
A: Medium is the message (makes sense to media theorists)  
B: Interfaces → cultural behaviours (makes sense to peopel in ITP) : 
        The Apple II project 
C: Environment → cultural behaviours (makes sense to architects) 
[A] Interfaces + [C] Environments (through physical placement + networked objects) → cultural behaviours

What underlies these things? Code!

How do the parts which are invisible to us (ex: code) in these equations and their materiality
specifically (hardware, and what software it can run) impact these things (environments, interfaces, toos)?
```

Sarah also encouraged me to look deeper at the impacts of code. If the basics of code can be seen as input → function → output, what kinds of inputs would lead to unexpected outcomes, and how would that effect culture? Meanwhile Nasif, offered more push back looking for specific examples. While _Programmed Environments_ makes sense, linking it to the Apple II felt like a stretch at the moment, but can work in the right context. In a sense, his thesis would fit in the theory.

I've also been re-reading books that help flesh out this theory. In Wendy Chun's work **Programmed Visions** she continuosly mentions the 'programmability' of things and how software encroaches on governance, media, and the fluid invisible/visible nature of software & code. Her work is highly influential, and helps suport _Programmed Environments_ as a direct way space has become something to be programmed. Meanwhile, **Code/Space** also goes int othe intangible nature of code and how it directly the way we move and exist in the physical world. The two works together are already under the [MIT Press Software Studies](https://mitpress.mit.edu/series/software-studies/?page_number=1) reading list. 

What makes programmability and _Programmed Environments_ interesting to me is how the introduction of code accelerated this wave of control over the physical world. The Apple II also helps back-up the claim. Through its preservation and simple uses, it existed in a time before code has as much control as it does now. Computer hardware was still limited that its impacts weren't as pervasive as they are now. 

For class, I briefly presented a new demo using the Apple II. Now that it can connect to WiFi networks, I created a simple program that allows me to stream my Macbook screen onto the old computer. While it mostly acts as a glorified monitor, it also presents a modified view of common interactions and subverts our expectations over how we view computer screens.
