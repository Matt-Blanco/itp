For this week, I began to piece together the conceptual framework I'll likely present as my thesis. It's broad and tries to cover a lot of ground, but I belive there's merit to the work, and it can help be the foundation of future (and more specific) interventions. Other midterms and finals took much of my attention so I wasn't as dedicated to my research.

While I've been enjoying working and tinkering with the Apple IIGS, it acts as a window into the past. After reading _The Soul of a New Machine_, I realize it'd be impossible to effectively explore the depths and inner working of a computer. Even during the 1980s, 16-bit computers were too complex for one person to understand. The term "computer" is also incredibly broad.

The history of computing is long and complicated, and to simplify it, my vintage computer acts as a window in time that I can directly interrogate rather than simply read about. 

Both my Apple II and Macbook Air are "computers" and yet the way they were used are completely different. In part due to their 1. hardware & software architecture, 2. available software, 3. network capabilities. Each of these facets are there own deep rabit holes and require different specialized knowledge, and it's why the questions I've been circling around are so complicated. To dissect what a "computer" is and what is "code" is different depending on the context.
