###### Week 2 - Mechanisms, Mechanisms, & Mechanisms

After last class we were introduced to all kinds of [mechanism](https://doubletake.notion.site/automatons?v=cf8b283c639b824abe6988882219b741&p=3dcb283c639b80d18acef144020006d4&pm=c). The class helped give the names for mechanisms and hardware I've seen before, but wasn't certain how they were implemented. 

For creating my own mechanism, I knew from the start I didn't want to deal with trying to cut gears without using the laser cutter or 3D printer. Though I did want to explore cams and how movement can be transferred from a crank to move an object in space.

From past examples it made sense how an object could move vertically or horizontally, so I wanted to make something simple that moved in three axes. Because mechanims are complex, I sketched my initial idea:  

<img class="img-fluide col-10" src="{{ 'assets/images/itp-blog/automata/week2/IMG_7705.jpg' | relative_url }}">

A crank would convert motion perpendicularly to a cam which would rotate an adhered dowel in a circle. Earlier in the week James had used hot glue around a cam which was able to create enough friction to rotate another cam.

First I found scrap material around the shop and junk shelf. Partially knolling the final materials and tools:
<img class="img-fluide col-10 my-2" src="{{ 'assets/images/itp-blog/automata/week2/IMG_7684.JPG' | relative_url }}">

Next, I assembled a simple box. The dimensions were rough, but it worked to assemble my mechanism. In order to have the simple rotating cam the box needed a central pillar to secure the cam. I also used a piece of card stock to reduce friction betweem the cam and thr box.
<img class="img-fluide col-10 my-2" src="{{ 'assets/images/itp-blog/automata/week2/IMG_7685.JPG' | relative_url }}">

I created a second layer on the cam so it would so there would be a stable base to attach a dowel to. With an angled force on the crank the cam turns better than I expected, though because of the unevenness the glue sometimes gets caught on the support bars.
<img class="img-fluide col-10 my-2" src="{{ 'assets/images/itp-blog/automata/week2/IMG_7687.JPG' | relative_url }}">

On the junk shelf I found some flexible wire and fluff so I thought it'd be fun and simple to have a small moving cloud. My other idea was using a halloween cauldron with a spinning spoon to "stir the pot"
<video src="https://www.mattblanco.me/itp/IMG_7688.MOV" class="col-8 mx-auto my-2" muted autoplay loop controls ></video>

Fabricating the mechanism was fun and simple, and it matched my sketch fairly well. The only thing I missed was the second layer of the cam. More precision tools would have made it work smoother, so for the rest of the classes I think I'll be working towards the fabrication of the mechanisms themselves than what they control.
